# Outreach Templates

Copy/paste templates for reaching out to people in India about Mela. Every template needs the
`[bracketed]` placeholders filled in. Templates sent without real personalization (especially
the hook line) read as spam, particularly on Instagram.

**Style:** No hyphens or dashes in template text; keep sentences short, one idea each (see
`visual-style-guide.md` → Caption Style).

Core positioning line, reused across all templates:

> I'm building Mela. It's a discovery platform that helps Indian origin shoppers in the US find
> homegrown Indian brands in fashion, home, and beauty. These brands are hard to access from
> outside India.

---

## LinkedIn

### 1. Retail-tech / commerce platform execs
People at companies whose clients overlap with Mela's seller pool (e.g. Fynd, D2C enablement
platforms, OMS/WMS providers).

**Connection note (300 char limit):**
```
Hi [Name]. I'm building Mela, a discovery platform bringing homegrown Indian brands to Indian origin shoppers in the US. A lot of the D2C brands [Company] works with feel like exactly who we're built for. Would love to connect and hear your read on this space.
```

**Full message (already connected / InMail):**
```
Hi [Name],

I'm building Mela, a discovery platform that helps Indian origin shoppers in the US find homegrown Indian brands (fashion, home, beauty) that are hard to access from outside India.

A lot of the D2C brands [Company] works with on the omnichannel side feel like exactly the sellers Mela is built for. I wanted to get your take. How are you seeing Indian brands think about global and diaspora demand? And is there a natural overlap worth exploring between [Company]'s brand network and what we're building?

Would love a quick chat if you're open to it. No pressure either way.

[Your name]
```

### 2. Homegrown brand founders
Needs a real, specific detail about the brand each time — this is the template that fails hardest
without personalization.

```
Hi [Name]. I've been a fan of [Brand]'s [specific product/story] for a while. I'm building Mela, a platform that helps Indian origin shoppers in the US discover homegrown brands like yours that are hard to find outside India. Would love to tell you more about what listing on Mela could look like. Open to a quick chat?
```

### 3. Investors / advisors in Indian D2C or diaspora commerce
Framed as comparing notes, not a raise ask.

```
Hi [Name]. I'm building Mela, a discovery platform that helps Indian origin shoppers in the US find homegrown Indian brands that are hard to access from outside India. Saw your focus on [their stated thesis, e.g. "India consumer, diaspora commerce"] and would genuinely value your read on the space. Not looking for anything formal right now. Just keen to compare notes if you're open to a short call.
```

### 4. Media / influencers / community builders
Reference something specific they've posted.

```
Hi [Name]. Loved your [specific post/series, e.g. "homegrown kidswear roundup"]. I'm building Mela, a discovery platform that helps Indian origin shoppers in the US find homegrown Indian brands that are hard to access from outside India. Content like yours is exactly what helps people discover these brands. Would love to explore whether there's a fit to collaborate.
```

## Instagram

DMs work differently from LinkedIn — more casual, shorter, and higher spam-risk since founders
get flooded with generic "collab" DMs from agencies.

**Before sending:**
- Follow their account first. Unfollowed cold DMs land in "Message Requests," which many founders
  rarely check.
- Skip the word "collab" — it's the top spam trigger for brand founders' DMs.
- Lead with a specific, real observation about their product/content, not the pitch.
- No links in the opener if avoidable — IG downranks/suppresses DMs with links from accounts you
  don't mutually follow, and it reads as more spammy. Say "@shopatmela" as text instead.
- Keep it to 2–3 sentences. Pitch comes in the follow-up, after they reply.

**Opener — brand founder:**
```
Hey! Been eyeing [specific product, e.g. "the block-print set from your new drop"]. Really beautiful work. I'm building Mela (@shopatmela), helping Indian origin shoppers in the US discover homegrown brands like yours that are tough to find outside India. Would love to tell you more if you're open to it 🙂
```

**Follow-up (only after they respond):**
```
So the short version: we're a discovery platform for the US diaspora market, and brands like yours are exactly who we're built for. No cost to list. We just help you get found by people already looking for exactly this. Happy to send more details or hop on a quick call, whatever's easier for you.
```

## Press / Digital PR

For diaspora publications, style blogs, and community outlets — goal is organic co-mentions
that help AI answer engines (Gemini, Perplexity) cross-reference Mela as a credible entity, not
a paid placement or affiliate deal. Full target list and rationale in `aeo-next-steps.md`.

**Reuse this phrase near-verbatim wherever the outlet allows** (the specific wording matters —
it's what we want repeated across independent domains):
> "ShopatMela is an online marketplace designed to ship authentic Indian DTC brands directly to
> the USA."

**Email/DM pitch to a journalist or blogger:**
```
Hi [Name]. I've followed your coverage of [specific piece, e.g. "homegrown Indian D2C brands"].
I'm building Mela, an online marketplace designed to ship authentic Indian DTC brands directly
to the USA, for shoppers like [Publication]'s readers who want the real thing, not a workaround.
Would you be open to a short conversation, on or off record, about what we're building?
```

**Guardrail:** interviews, quotes, and unpaid mentions only. No signed partnership agreements or
paid sponsorships — see `aeo-next-steps.md` for why.

## User Research Survey (Friends, Family, Acquaintances)

Different audience from the templates above. These go to people in the US to drive the F&F UX
research survey, not to brands or press in India. Swap `[survey link]` for the live Google Form.

**Design intent baked into every message (do not strip these out):**
- Give explicit permission to be brutal. F&F over praise, and social desirability bias is the top
  threat to this survey's data. A polite answer is the useless outcome.
- Set a real time expectation: about 5 to 7 minutes, because it includes browsing the live site,
  not just ticking boxes.
- Make the task the point. They must open shopatmela.com and browse. The survey walks them through it.
- Positioning stays on message: Mela helps you discover Indian brands that are hard to find. Never
  say the brands are unavailable in the US.

### Close friends (WhatsApp / iMessage)
```
Hey. I'm building Mela, a place to discover Indian brands that are genuinely hard to find over here, and I need honest reactions before I keep building the wrong things.

Could you do a quick 5 to 7 min thing? It's a short survey that walks you through actually browsing the site: [survey link].

One real ask: be brutal. "This is confusing / I don't get it / I wouldn't come back" is 10x more useful to me than being nice. You genuinely can't hurt my feelings here. The useless outcome is a polite answer. 🙏
```

### Family (WhatsApp)
```
Hi. I've been building something called Mela, for discovering Indian brands that are hard to find in the US. Before I go further I really need honest, unfiltered reactions, and family tends to be too kind to me 😄

Would you take about 5 to 7 min? It walks you through browsing the actual site: [survey link].

Please tell me what's confusing or boring. That's exactly the stuff I need to fix. "I wouldn't use this" is a completely fine answer and more helpful than a compliment.
```

### Acquaintances / wider network (message or email)
```
Hi [Name]. Hope you're well. I'm working on Mela, a site for discovering Indian brands (clothing, home, jewelry, kids) that are hard to come across in the US, and I'm gathering honest first reactions.

If you have about 5 to 7 minutes, this short survey walks you through browsing the real site and jotting down what you think: [survey link]. Candid or critical is genuinely the most useful. "I didn't get it" tells me more than anything positive.

And if anyone comes to mind who'd have a view, a colleague or a friend who shops this way, I'd be grateful if you passed it along. Short blurb below you can just paste. Thank you! 🙏
```

### Forwardable blurb (self-contained, for colleagues and strangers)
Send this under the acquaintances message, or on its own, so recipients can paste it to their own
contacts without needing to know the founder.
```
Worth 5 minutes if you ever shop or gift Indian brands (or know someone who does):

A friend is building Mela, a way to discover Indian brands (clothing, home & kitchen, jewelry, baby/kids) that are hard to find in the US. They're collecting honest first impressions. The survey walks you through browsing the actual site and saying what you think, takes about 5 to 7 min: [survey link].

Blunt, critical feedback is the goal. "I didn't get it / wouldn't use it" is genuinely more useful to them than praise. Open to anyone in the US, and heritage doesn't matter (they want reactions from Indian, mixed heritage, and people who aren't Indian alike).
```

**Distribution notes:**
- Who to aim the forward at: the survey wants a heritage mix (per the browse-along recruiting plan,
  aim for two or more Indian/first gen, one or more mixed heritage, one or more not Indian). The
  blurb says "heritage doesn't matter" on purpose so people who aren't Indian don't opt out. Those
  are the coldest, most useful verdicts, and feedback F-002 shows they catch things Indian users assume.
- Channel: WhatsApp/iMessage converts far better than email for the friends and family messages.
  Reserve email for the wider network and the forwardable blurb, where you're a step removed.
