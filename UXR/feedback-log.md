# Mela UXR Feedback Log

## Purpose
This log is the single source of truth for user feedback on Mela — what people said, what it means, and what we did about it. Every piece of feedback that informs a product decision should live here.

## How to add an entry
1. Copy any existing row and paste it at the bottom of the table.
2. Increment the ID (`F-001` → `F-002`, etc.).
3. Fill every column — leave no cell blank. Use `—` only if a field genuinely doesn't apply yet (e.g. Linked change before the fix is merged).
4. Update the **Current theme** section above the table if the new entry shifts or sharpens the synthesis.

**Status values:**
- `Planned` — acknowledged, a fix is scoped but not started
- `Actioned` — a concrete change was made
- `Watching` — noted; not acting now but tracking for signal strength
- `Ignored` — consciously deprioritized; reason must be in the Action column

## Current theme (updated 2026-07-06)

All three pieces of feedback converge on a single root cause: Mela presents as a generic product catalog rather than a curated cultural destination, and nothing on the homepage — layout, imagery, typography, or copy — signals that this is an Indian brand discovery experience. The problem operates at two levels: **structural** (a grid of listings without editorial curation or a discovery narrative) and **aesthetic** (no visual or typographic cues that anchor the brand in India). Until both levels are addressed, first-time visitors have no reason to stay, explore, or understand what makes Mela different from a standard marketplace.

**Update (2026-08-17) — first counter-signal on the render problem.** Jasmeet (F-008) — a first-gen Indian, 10+ years in the US, Bay Area/tech (the *same diaspora segment as F-001*, and Neha's demographic) — described the homepage *unprompted* as "Indian brands, their stories & the narratives behind them" and "very different — expected a search page or a homepage." That is the differentiation *rendering* — the opposite of what F-001–F-003 reported from the same kind of user, which is what makes it worth watching. It's one data point, so treat it as provisional; but if it repeats across cold respondents, the primary bottleneck moves **downstream of first impression to discovery completion**: catalog breadth for gifting (F-009), trust-eroding "best seller" labels on a thin catalog (F-010), and the absence of occasion/recipient-guided gifting that leaves the occasion-shopper in an unguided 5–10-min search before dropping off (F-011). Watch for repetition before re-scoping.

**Update (2026-08-19) — the counter-signal repeated, and it's no longer one data point.** A second first-gen diaspora respondent (F-012, cousin, 20+ years in the US) independently read the differentiation as landing — "feels more authenticated and catered… includes reputed brands," favorable *against* Amazon ("no verified buyers") and Etsy — found a kurti she'd realistically buy, rated the brands "few or none" findable elsewhere, and gave strong return + referral intent (F-015). Two of two first-gen respondents now report render + curation working for their segment, which firms up the **downstream-bottleneck** read. The emerging downstream blockers, in rough priority: (1) **total-cost / tariff transparency** — she's been burned by surprise duties elsewhere and won't buy without knowing if tariff is included (F-013), the sharpest actionable item yet and squarely Neha's total-cost-clarity trust need; (2) **catalog breadth** — recurring across F-009 (gifting) and F-014 (home/bedsheets); (3) guided gifting (F-011). Caveat: both positive respondents are warm (cousin, friend) and in-segment — the cold, non-diaspora verdict is still missing and matters most.

**Update (2026-09-03) — new blocker surfaces: Add to Cart isn't a cart, and the duty/tariff concern is now a repeated pattern.** Two more respondents, Ranjini (lawyer, mid-30s, 1st-gen, married, no kids) and Megha (wife's cousin, 2nd-gen, 40s, married, 2 kids), add a distinct downstream friction point not previously logged: **"Add to Cart" doesn't behave like a cart** — it saves the item to "liked," then redirects out to the vendor's own site to actually purchase; Ranjini hit an out-of-stock item only after that redirect (F-020), and separately flagged that she hasn't been able to see shipping costs before checkout, which could deter purchase if high (F-022). Megha's unprompted pricing/duty question ("Pricing compared to cost to purchase in India, duty?", F-030) is the *third* respondent (after F-013) to raise total-cost transparency — this is now a repeated pattern, not a one-off, and should move up in priority. Both respondents also reinforce the occasion/recipient-guided gifting gap (F-011): Ranjini couldn't find gifts organized by occasion (F-025), and Megha wants curated Diwali gift hampers and occasion-based categories (F-031, F-032). Ranjini additionally flagged a category mis-tagging bug (Rugs & floor coverings surfaces rings, F-021), redundant "All Categories" navigation (F-017), and a feedback popup that blocks page interaction before it's answered (F-018).
