# Mela Browse-Along Session Guide

> **What this is:** the moderator guide for the post-survey moderated browse-alongs — the *actual* test of Mela's core bet. The F&F survey is the net; these 5–7 sessions are the answer. Participants come from the survey's Q14 opt-ins ("15-minute screen-share where you browse and think out loud").
>
> **Created:** Jul 2026. Owner: UXR lead. Related: [[feedback-log]] (F-001–F-003), [[buyer-personas]].

---

## 1. What these sessions actually test

Mela's differentiation lives in the **experience layer** (curated discovery of Indian brands), not the inventory layer (the brands are hard to find, not unavailable). That makes Mela Depop-shaped, not eBay-shaped: the curation/discovery *is* the product-market-fit question, not a detail to iterate later. See the strategy thread — the catalog is explicitly not the moat.

Two questions, in priority order:

1. **Does the differentiation *render*?** In the first 10–20 seconds, does the person perceive a curated Indian-discovery *destination* — or a generic product store? (This is the F-001–F-003 test, observed live instead of inferred from survey text.)
2. **Does discovery *land*?** Over the session, do they move from "browsing" to "I found something I didn't know I wanted, and I'd be disappointed to lose this"?

**The confound these sessions exist to resolve.** A weak survey result is ambiguous between two opposite diagnoses, and only live observation can separate them:

- **(a) No demand** — people don't want curated Indian-brand discovery → kill or pivot.
- **(b) Demand exists, but this build doesn't render the differentiation** — the discovery experience that would create fit isn't present yet → concept could fit; this build doesn't show it.

Everything below is designed to tell (a) from (b). **If you learn only one thing per session, learn which of these you're looking at.**

## 2. Logistics

- **Who:** 5–7 participants from Q14 opt-ins. Deliberately span heritage: aim for ≥2 Indian/first-gen, ≥1 mixed-heritage, ≥1 non-Indian. Heritage changes what "renders" means (a non-Indian participant won't decode Indian visual cues the way a first-gen one will — that's signal, not noise).
- **Length:** book **30-minute** slots; tell them **15–20**. The buffer is for setup and the think-aloud running long. Always ask permission before going over.
- **Format:** video call, participant shares their screen and browses on their own device. You watch and listen; you do **not** drive.
- **Recording:** get **verbal consent to record screen + audio** at the top. Screen and voice only — face optional. State it's for your notes, won't be shared. (Ethics note: these are friends giving candid opinions on your venture — keep recordings private, delete after synthesis.)
- **Device:** let them use whatever they'd really use. If they'd normally shop on phone, have them share phone screen — mobile is Mela's primary discovery surface and where the known layout issues live.

---

## 3. Before each session (10 min prep)

- **Pre-read their survey response.** Tag them: heritage (Q3a), did they find something (Q4), disappointment (Q6), and their first-impression text (QA). Walk in knowing whether you're talking to a "found it / would miss it" or a "nothing / meh" person — and probe the *gap* between what they wrote and what you see them do.
- **Set your own discipline.** The two hardest moments are (1) staying silent during the first-impression look, and (2) not rescuing them when they're stuck. Both silences are data. Sitting on your hands is the job.
- **Bias toward watching, not asking.** Friends will be generous in words. Behavior — where the cursor goes, when they lean in, when they go quiet, whether they check a price elsewhere — is far harder to fake. When statement and behavior conflict, trust behavior.

## 4. Session flow

### 0 · Consent & setup — 2 min
Get recording consent. "There are no right answers, and you can't hurt my feelings — the most useful thing you can do is say exactly what you're thinking, including when it's confusing or boring. I'm going to stay quiet a lot; that's on purpose."

### 1 · Warm-up: their world, no Mela yet — 3–4 min
Ground them before they see the site.
- "Last time you bought something Indian — clothes, home, a gift, anything — walk me through how that went."
- "Where do you even start when you want something like that?"
- *Listen for:* fulfillment mode (knows the brand/item) vs inspiration mode (loose intent). This predicts how they'll use Mela and frames everything after.

### 2 · The cold open — the first-impression test — 5 min ★
**The single most important part. Do not rush it, do not narrate over it.**
- "I'm going to send a link. When it loads, just **look — don't click or scroll — for about 15 seconds.** Then tell me what you're seeing." Send the link.
- Stay silent through the look. Watch their eyes/cursor: what do they land on first?
- Then, unprompted and neutral: **"What is this? Who's it for?"** → then **"How's it different, if at all, from Amazon or Etsy?"**
- *Do NOT ask* "does it feel curated / does it feel Indian?" — naming the construct hands them the answer. The whole test is whether they say it *without* prompting.
- **This is the render test.** Code it: did India / discovery / curation / "a destination with a point of view" come up on their own — or did they describe "an online store that sells clothes and stuff"?

### 3 · The find task, think-aloud — 10–12 min ★
- "Now browse like you actually would, and try to find one specific thing you'd genuinely consider buying — for yourself, someone in your family, or a gift. **Keep talking the whole time** — tell me what you're looking at and what you're thinking, even the boring parts."
- **Go quiet.** Let them drive. When they pause, "What are you thinking right now?" — never "try the collections" or any steer.
- If they stall out completely for ~30s, note where, then: "What were you hoping to find here?"
- *Watch for (see §5 checklist):* which surface they use (search vs category vs curated collection vs just scrolling), the moment of "oh" vs the moment they go flat, whether they price-check elsewhere, whether they ever express surprise/discovery.

### 4 · The discovery-vs-inventory probe — 3 min
The live version of survey Q11 — separates "the catalog is the value" from "the curation is the value."
- About something they found or saw: "Could you have found this on Amazon or with a quick Google — or did this feel like something you'd only come across here?"
- "What made you say that?"
- *This is your clearest read on whether the moat is catalog or experience.*

### 5 · Return & wrap — 3 min
- "Realistically — when do you next picture yourself opening something like this? What would have to be here for that visit to be worth it?" (return trigger; occasion-anchored)
- "If this disappeared tomorrow, honestly — would you notice?" (spoken Sean-Ellis; read the *hesitation*, not just the word)
- "Anyone you'd actually send this to? Who?"

## 5. Observation checklist — what to watch (not ask)

| Watch for | Reads as |
|---|---|
| First fixation on load | Where the design pulls the eye — hero/story vs product grid |
| Unprompted mention of India / discovery / curation in §2 | **Differentiation renders** (evidence vs diagnosis (b)) |
| "It's like an online store / like Amazon" in §2 | Differentiation **not** rendering — you're testing eBay-Mela |
| Which surface they reach for in §3 | Curated-collection use = discovery layer found; pure search/scroll = it isn't surfacing |
| A visible "oh —" / lean-in / "that's nice" | Discovery landing |
| Going quiet, flat scrolling, "hm, I don't know" | Discovery *not* landing — note exactly where |
| Opening a new tab to check a price (esp. first-gen) | Neha price-scrutiny live; watch the reaction to the number |
| "I wouldn't know if this is authentic" / hesitation | Trust gap (Priya/Sarah/Arun) |
| Statement/behavior conflict (says "love it," acts bored) | Trust the behavior |

## 6. Non-leading probe bank

Use these; avoid anything that plants the answer.

- "What are you thinking right now?"
- "What made you stop there?"
- "What were you expecting to happen?"
- "Say more about that."
- "What would you normally do next?"
- Silence. (Count to five before filling it.)

**Avoid:** "Do you like the design?" · "Doesn't this feel curated?" · "Did you notice the collections?" · anything with "Indian" *you* introduce before they do · any question answerable with a polite yes.

---

## 7. Signals: evidence for vs against the bet

After each session, place it:

**Evidence the Depop-bet is alive (differentiation renders + discovery lands):**
- Unprompted "curated / discovery / a place for Indian brands" in §2
- Reaches a curated surface and reacts to it
- A genuine "oh, I didn't know I wanted this" moment
- §4: "I'd only find this here"
- §5: names a concrete return occasion, hesitates on "would you notice?"

**Evidence for (b) — demand real but build under-renders:**
- Wants Indian-brand discovery (warm-up shows the appetite) BUT §2 reads it as a generic store AND §3 they never find the discovery layer
- → *the concept isn't disproven; this build hasn't shown it.* Points at homepage/curation work, not a kill.

**Evidence for (a) — no demand:**
- Even on a genuinely curated, well-presented path, the discovery job doesn't matter to them — they'd rather just search/buy, or don't want the category
- Consistent across heritage segments
- → *the harder signal; weight it heavily precisely because it's the one that hurts.*

## 9. After 5–7 sessions — synthesis & decision

Don't average. Sort every session into (a) / (b) / bet-alive, then read the pattern:

- **Mostly "bet-alive":** discovery renders and lands for a real segment → the homepage under-render (F-001–F-003) is a *conversion tax on a real product*, not an existential problem. Fix the render; you have a fit signal.
- **Mostly (b):** the appetite is there but the current build hides the differentiation → **do not read this as a PMF failure.** The next move is the curated-slice test (a hand-built, high-editorial collection) to see if discovery lands when it's actually presented — *then* re-judge PMF. You haven't tested the hypothesis until the differentiation is on screen.
- **Mostly (a), across heritage segments:** the discovery job isn't wanted, even when rendered → the honest hard signal. Weight it heavily and reconsider the bet before more build.

**Cross-check against the survey**, don't replace it: the sessions explain *why* the survey's QA absence rate and Q11 split came out the way they did. Converging session + survey evidence on the same diagnosis is worth far more than either alone.

**Remember the sample's limits** (see [[buyer-personas]] Known Gaps): friends, affluent, socially obligated, parenthood-heavy. The strangers who'd give the coldest, most useful verdict aren't here. A warm result is suggestive, not proof; a *cold* result from a friendly sample is the more trustworthy signal.
